Break the narration below into visual scenes for a documentary, at
roughly {scenes_per_minute} scenes per minute of narration.

Established visual bible for this episode (already locked in -- use it,
don't invent different architecture/clothing/materials):
{visual_bible_summary}

For each scene return: scene_id, duration (seconds), narration (the exact
text spoken during this scene), location, period, characters, objects,
camera (one of: zoom_in, zoom_out, pan_left, pan_right, pan_up, pan_down,
slow_push, slow_pull), transition (e.g. crossfade), and visual_focus.

visual_focus is the single most important field for image generation: a
short (10-18 word) concrete description of the main subject and what it's
doing/showing in THIS SPECIFIC scene, written like an image-generation
prompt fragment -- NOT a restatement or summary of the narration, and NOT
generic. Requirements:
- Must describe something that ONLY belongs to this scene's narration
  segment, not something that could apply to any scene in the episode.
- Must incorporate the established clothing/attire from the visual bible
  above when a person is shown -- do not leave attire unspecified or
  invent different clothing than what's locked in.
- Use concrete nouns and visible details (a person, an object, an action,
  a setting feature), not abstract ideas, historical claims, or anything
  that can't be literally seen in a single still image.
- ONE clear subject/action, not a list of several things happening at
  once -- the image model generating from this has weak prompt-following.

Bad visual_focus (abstract, not a visual subject):
"The importance of trade routes connecting West Africa to the wider
world through Saharan commerce."

Bad visual_focus (generic, could be any scene, ignores established attire):
"a person standing in a historical setting"

Good visual_focus (one concrete subject and action, specific to this
scene's narration, using the established attire):
"a camel caravan crossing dunes at dusk, drivers in indigo-dyed robes,
loaded with salt slabs"

Narration:
{narration_text}

Return a JSON array of scene objects.
